The school day is covered. The rest is up to us.

Since September, schools in England are expected to be mobile phone-free by default, and Ofsted now checks phone policies at every inspection. Most parents welcome it. But school rules stop at the gate. The walk in, the bus home, the evening and bedtime are still ours to sort out, and a lot of families are reaching for a brick phone.

The trouble with a brick phone

A basic phone does calls and texts well, and the battery lasts for days. For some children that's exactly right. For many 11 and 12-year-olds, it means:

The questions parents actually ask

Won't they find a way round it?

A determined child might, and any app that promises otherwise isn't being straight with you. Brickify uses the phone's own system-level controls: Apple's Family Controls on iPhone, and Android's accessibility service, plus optional Device Owner mode, on Android. Setup walks you through closing the side doors, and you get an email if protection is removed.

Known limitations and mitigations →

Isn't this just Screen Time with extra steps?

Not quite. On iPhone, Brickify is built on the same Apple framework as Screen Time, and on Android it uses Android's accessibility service and optionally Device Owner. These make it hard to get round. What Brickify adds: several profiles that swap themselves through the week, a PIN plus second factor, alerts, a guided setup, and the same app on Android.

My child will be the only one.

Probably not. According to Smartphone Free Childhood, more than 200,000 UK children are now in its Parent Pact, covering over half of UK schools. And a Brickified phone doesn't announce itself. It looks like any other phone; fewer apps work at certain times, that's all.

On the same side as the movement

Brickify isn't a rival to brick phones or to Smartphone Free Childhood. If you've signed the Pact and can wait until 14, wait. That's a good choice. Brickify is for families who've decided a phone is needed now, and for children who already have a smartphone that needs to be quieter. At 14, it helps too: instead of going from nothing to everything overnight, open one app at a time.
